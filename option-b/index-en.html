What is eDNA and how does it work?+

eDNA (environmental DNA) is genetic material that organisms leave in their surroundings. By analysing it, we can identify which species are present without catching or directly observing them.

What samples can be collected?+

We analyse water (lakes, rivers and seas), snow (mammal tracks), soil and sediment. Your staff can collect samples using our kits and instructions.

Is eDNA cost-effective?+

Yes — collecting a sample takes minutes rather than days, and a single analysis can identify multiple species. This makes the method more cost-effective, especially for large-scale monitoring.

Can eDNA estimate population sizes?+

Yes, quantitative PCR can provide indications of population size. Microsatellite analysis can identify individual animals.

How long does it take to get results?+

Straightforward qPCR analyses: 2–4 weeks. Comprehensive metabarcoding projects: 4–8 weeks from receiving samples to the final report.

Can we collect samples ourselves?+

Yes, we provide sampling kits and detailed instructions. We also offer training, or our field team can collect the samples for you.
