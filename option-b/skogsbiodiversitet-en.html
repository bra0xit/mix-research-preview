MIX RESEARCH / SERVICE NOTE

Forest biodiversity assessment

Map fungal and bacterial communities in soil samples to assess a forest’s biodiversity value.

01SERVICE
02THE QUESTION

Map the hidden life beneath the soil

Much of a forest’s biodiversity lies underground — fungi, bacteria and microorganisms essential to ecosystem function. With eDNA metagenomics, we can map these invisible communities and build a comprehensive picture of forest biodiversity.

  • Mycorrhizal fungi — essential to forest health and tree growth
  • Wood-inhabiting fungi — indicators of old forests with high conservation value
  • Red-listed fungal species that are difficult to find in conventional surveys
  • Soil bacteria — indicators of soil quality and ecological status
  • Pathogens — early detection of disease, such as fungi associated with spruce bark beetles
  • Forest biodiversity assessment — complements conventional methods
  • Comparing forests before harvesting or conservation
  • Monitoring forestry practices and conservation measures
  • Certification and sustainability reporting (FSC, PEFC)
  • Research projects on soil ecology and climate impacts
